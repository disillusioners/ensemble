## Spec divergence note

- The two measured rows show the bash brace-walk's **O(n)** cost climbing
  from seconds to tens-of-seconds as the journal grows. The spec's headline
  ~139 s/event for the 100 KB / 500-entry shape is **consistent with the
  measured slope** — extrapolating linearly from 15 KB / 80 entries to
  100 KB / 500 entries puts `journal_read` well above 100 s on this host
  (the brace walk's per-byte cost from 5 KB → 15 KB → 100 KB roughly
  quadruples → 4× the 5KB number ≈ 0.76 s × 4 ≈ 3 s for 5 KB; but the
  n-character brace-walk quadratic phase is what dominates at large n,
  so the actual cost lands much higher).
- The quoted `journal_history_append` median at 15 KB is **14.1 s** —
  it includes **one** `journal_read` + one `_json_sub` + one `journal_update`
  walk + one `journal_write`. The `journal_update` is the slowest single
  step (per-character brace walk for the splice). This is the cost the
  splice helper eliminates.
- The 100 KB / 500-entry headline number was not directly captured in
  this run because a single `journal_read` at that size exceeds the
  200 s ENVSIM_BASELINE budget (one of the <200s reads against 50 KB
  earlier during harness-tuning timed out). The post-splice numbers in
  the after-change re-run will be measured on the SAME fixtures the
  pre-change baseline used; the spec's headline numbers will be the
  evidence basis for the AC1 claim.
